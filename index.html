Master kanji pronunciation

Every kanji has on'yomi (音読み, Chinese-based) and often kun'yomi (訓読み, native Japanese) readings. Click a kanji to see its readings, when to use each one, example words and sentences.

How to know which reading to use

There's no rule that works 100% of the time, but these patterns cover the vast majority of words you'll meet. Learn them, then learn the exceptions as vocabulary.

Is the kanji followed by hiragana that's part of the word (okurigana)?
e.g. 食べる, 大きい, 見る
→ Almost always kun'yomi
Is the kanji standing alone as a word?
e.g. 山, 水, 人, 車
→ Usually kun'yomi (exceptions: 本 ほん, 金 きん, 気 き)
Is it part of a compound of two or more kanji with no hiragana?
e.g. 学生, 電車, 来週
→ Usually on'yomi (exceptions: native compounds like 花見, 名前)
Is it a person's name or place name?
e.g. 山本, 品川
→ Anything goes — names often use kun'yomi, just memorise them

音 On'yomi 音読み

Readings borrowed from Chinese when kanji came to Japan. Dictionaries write them in katakana.

  • Mostly in compounds (熟語): 学生 がくせい, 電話 でんわ
  • Usually short: 1–2 morae like ニチ, ガク, デン
  • Numbers with counters: 三時 さんじ, 五人 ごにん
  • A kanji may have several on'yomi from different eras: 生 → セイ / ショウ

訓 Kun'yomi 訓読み

The native Japanese word that was matched to the kanji's meaning. Written in hiragana.

  • Kanji used alone: 山 やま, 水 みず
  • Verbs & adjectives with okurigana: 食べる たべる, 大きい おおきい
  • The okurigana tells you which kun'yomi: 上げる あげる vs 上る のぼる
  • Native counting: 一つ ひとつ, 二つ ふたつ

混 Mixed readings

Some compounds mix the two types.

  • 重箱読み (jūbako) — on + kun: 本屋 ほん・や, 毎朝 まい・あさ
  • 湯桶読み (yutō) — kun + on: 見本 み・ほん, 場所 ば・しょ

特 Special readings 熟字訓

The whole word has a reading that can't be split per kanji. Just learn them as vocabulary.

  • 今日 きょう (today), 明日 あした (tomorrow)
  • 大人 おとな (adult), 一人 ひとり, 二人 ふたり
  • 時計 とけい (clock), お土産 おみやげ (souvenir)
  • 上手 じょうず, 下手 へた

🔊 Sound changes

Readings change slightly when kanji join together. Once you know these, many "weird" readings make sense.

Rendaku 連濁

The first sound of the 2nd part becomes voiced (か→が, ひ→び, さ→ざ…)

花 はな + 火 ひ → 花火 はなび
小 お + 川 かわ → 小川 おがわ
日曜 + 日 ひ → 日曜日 にちようび

Gemination 促音便

A final く/ち/つ shrinks into a small っ before k, s, t, p sounds

学 がく + 校 こう → がっこう
一 いち + 緒 しょ → いっしょ
一 いち + 本 ほん → いっぽん

Counters h → p/b

は/ひ/ふ/へ/ほ counters change after 1, 3, 6, 8, 10 and 何

一本 いっぽん
二本 にほん
三本 さんぼん

📅 Counting days — a classic trap

Days of the month 2–10 use native numbers + か; from 11 on (except 14, 20, 24) they use on'yomi + にち.
